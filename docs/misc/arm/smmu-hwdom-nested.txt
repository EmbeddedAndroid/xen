Hardware domain stage 1 on an Arm SMMUv2 (dom0-iommu=nested)
=============================================================

Without this option Xen drives every SMMUv2 it can initialise with stage-2
context banks only and describes it to the hardware domain disabled. The
hardware domain's devices then translate through Xen's stage 2 of it, but
the hardware domain gets no IOMMU of its own, and Linux drivers that need
an IOMMU domain for their own address space (msm DPU and Adreno, fastrpc,
video codecs) do not probe or need bounce buffers below 4 GiB.

With dom0-iommu=nested, a direct-mapped hardware domain drives stage 1 of
each SMMUv2 that implements nested translation (ID0.NTS), on top of Xen's
stage 2 of the hardware domain:

- Xen splits the stream match registers and context banks it may use (the
  first 128 SMRs at most, below any slice the secure firmware owns, see
  smmu-secure-firmware.txt): the hardware domain gets the bottom 7/8 of
  each, Xen keeps the rest (at least two of each) for its stage-2 bank of
  the hardware domain and for other domains' devices.
- The SMMU node is described to the hardware domain enabled. Xen maps it
  the context bank pages of its own banks and their interrupts, and
  emulates the global register pages: ID0/ID1 describe a stage-1 only
  SMMU with the hardware domain's SMRs and context banks, sCR0 and sACR
  are the hardware domain's own copies, and only the all-non-secure TLB
  invalidation, the global sync and the global fault status reach the
  hardware.
- Every context bank of the hardware domain is a stage-1 bank nested on
  Xen's stage-2 bank (CBAR type 3), so its translations, and its page
  table walks, go through Xen's stage 2 of the hardware domain. A bypass
  stream translates through the stage-2 bank; a stream routed to a bank
  that is not the hardware domain's faults.
- An SMR that could match a stream of another domain or of the secure
  firmware is left invalid and logged; Xen refuses to assign another
  domain a device whose streams match a valid SMR of the hardware domain.
  Refused SMR and S2CR values read back as what is in effect.

Xen's own stage 2 of the hardware domain shares the CPU's page tables, as
for any other domain, so grant and foreign mappings and memory lent to
the secure world are reflected in what the hardware domain's devices can
reach.

Not handled: SMMUs Xen cannot initialise at boot (for instance one in a
power domain that is off) stay passed through as before; extended stream
IDs are hidden from the hardware domain.
